How high you'll be, day by day

Two treks, two peaks. The Bumdra camp on 4 October is what makes the Mardi Himal ridge a week later feel manageable.

Read the two peaks together. You sleep at 3,800 m at Bumdra on 4 October and touch about 3,850 m the next morning above Tiger's Nest. Six days later you sleep at 3,580 m at High Camp — lower than you already slept in Bhutan — before the push to 4,200 m. That ordering is the whole reason this trek is a sensible thing to bolt onto the end of the trip rather than a gamble.

Watch the shape of the 10th and 11th. On the 10th you touch 3,900 m in the afternoon and drop back to sleep at 3,580 m; on the 11th you go to 4,200 m (4,500 m with Base Camp) and sleep at 3,210 m. Climb high, sleep low, twice in a row — that is what the sixth day bought you.

The big valley day — Boudha, Swayambhu, Pashupati arrange a car + guide

One full day is enough for the three that matter, if you run them in the right order and take a car with a driver for the whole day (~€35–45 for 8–10 h, ask the hotel). A licensed guide for the morning adds a lot — Kathmandu's temples are dense with meaning you will not read off the stones.

  • 07:00 · Boudhanath. Go early. Walk the kora clockwise with Tibetan pilgrims while the butter lamps are still lit and the crowds haven't arrived, then breakfast on a rooftop looking at the eyes on the stupa. This is the single best hour of the Kathmandu leg.
  • 10:00 · Swayambhunath. The Monkey Temple on its hill — 365 steps if you want them, or drive round to the back entrance. Best valley panorama in the city. Keep a hand on your sunglasses; the macaques are professionals.
  • 12:30 · Kathmandu Durbar Square & Thamel. Lunch, the Kumari Ghar (the living goddess's house), and the last of the Indra Jatra scaffolding. Do a first pass through Thamel to price gear you might still want for the trek — you can buy the missing bits here or in Pokhara.
  • 17:15 · Pashupatinath. The Bagmati cremation ghats and the evening aarti around 18:00–19:00: bells, fire, chanting, dusk. Non-Hindus can't enter the main temple, but the east bank terraces are where you want to be anyway. Do not photograph the cremations.

Before bed: repack. You are flying to Bhutan in the morning and coming back to Kathmandu on 6 October only to transit to Pokhara. Decide tonight what goes to Bhutan, what goes on the trek, and what stays behind in storage — see the luggage plan on the Practical page.

The Mardi Himal trek

Wed 7 – Mon 12 Oct · 6 walking days

The route in one paragraph

Mardi Himal is the quiet cousin of the Annapurna circuit routes: a single high ridge that climbs out of subtropical forest onto an open alpine spine directly beneath Machhapuchhre. You gain about 2,700 m over five days and lose it in one. There is no technical ground — it is steep, rooty forest walking at the bottom and a bare, exposed crest at the top. Because you'll have slept at 3,800 m at Bumdra just three days earlier, you arrive far better acclimatised than most people who start this trek.

Trek 2

Out of the trees — Low Camp

  • Three to four hours of ridge forest through Rest Camp to Low Camp (2,970 m). The canopy thins as you go and the rhododendrons get gnarled and wind-bent.
  • Low Camp is where the mountain finally shows itself: Machhapuchhre fills the entire northern sky, unreasonably close, and Annapurna South and Hiunchuli sit to the left. Get there by early afternoon, before the cloud builds.
  • Do nothing with the afternoon. In the five-day version this is where you'd push further up the ridge to acclimatise; you no longer need to. Tea, a book, the view, deep-fried Tibetan bread, dal bhat, bed by nine.

Altitude from here on. Sleep low, walk high, drink 3–4 litres a day, and tell your guide about any headache rather than toughing it out. You've been at 3,800 m recently, which helps a great deal — but Bumdra was one night, not a week.

Trek 3

The short one — up onto the spine to Badal Danda the added day

  • Two to three hours and only +240 m, but the landscape changes completely. The forest gives out around 3,200 m and you step onto a narrow open crest with drops on both sides, dwarf rhododendron underfoot and the whole Annapurna wall ahead of you.
  • Badal Danda means "cloud hill", and it earns the name: you are now at the height where the afternoon cloud sea forms below you. Three or four teahouses on a grassy shoulder, and in our view the most beautiful place to sleep on the whole route — better views than Low Camp, a fraction of the traffic of High Camp.
  • Arrive before noon and you get the whole afternoon on the ridge. Walk twenty minutes up and sit. Watch the cloud come in and go out again. This is the day the trek stops being a march and becomes a holiday.
  • Early night, because tomorrow is short too and there is no reason to be tired for the day after.

Trek 4

High Camp by lunch, then climb high and sleep low

  • A two-hour morning along the crest to High Camp (3,580 m), the last permanent teahouse on the route. Basic rooms, shared everything, a dining hut with a stove, and one of the great teahouse views in Nepal. You'll be there for lunch.

Trek 5 — the big one

Upper View Point, 4,200 m — and Base Camp if the day allows

  • 04:30 · Head torches on. Climb the ridge in the dark, steeply at first, then along the bare spine you walked yesterday afternoon — which you will be very glad of.
  • ~06:00 · View Point (3,900 m) for sunrise: Machhapuchhre catches first, then Annapurna South and Hiunchuli, then the whole wall. On the other side, the Pokhara valley is a lake of cloud with hills poking through.
  • ~08:00 · Upper View Point (4,200 m) — the trip's summit in every sense. Machhapuchhre's twin fishtail summit is ~2 km away and 2.5 km above you, close enough to see the flutings in the ice.
  • Optional: Mardi Himal Base Camp (4,500 m), another two hours out and back over easy but airy ground. You are better placed to take it than you would have been on the five-day plan — an extra night of acclimatisation and a shorter day behind you. Your guide will still call it on the morning.
  • Then down to High Camp for a late breakfast and on to Badal Danda (3,210 m) for a second night in the best beds on the ridge.

Weather rule. If the morning is socked in, don't burn yourself out — go as far as View Point, come back, and decide over breakfast. You still have 13 October as slack at the bottom, and unlike the five-day plan you have already banked yesterday afternoon's view.

Nepal 8

Pokhara — recovery, and the buffer keep it loose

This day now does two jobs: it's your recovery day and your slack. Do not set an alarm, then pick from this list — or do none of it, if the trek ran long and you're only here because the mountain gave you an extra day.

  • Paragliding from Sarangkot — one of the top three sites on earth for it, and October is the start of the good season. Thirty minutes tandem runs roughly US$90–100, an hour US$110–150. Fly between 09:00 and 12:00; only with an NCAA-licensed operator and a CIVL-certified pilot. Landing is at the lakeshore.
  • Row yourself across Phewa Lake to Tal Barahi temple on its island, then on to the far shore and the 45-minute climb up to the World Peace Pagoda. Best late afternoon; come down by taxi.
  • An hour of Ayurvedic massage in Lakeside, ~NPR 2,500–4,000. Your calves will file a formal complaint otherwise.
  • The International Mountain Museum — much better than it sounds, especially the day after a trek: Himalayan mountaineering history, the ethnography of the hill peoples, a full Sherpa house.
  • Begnas Lake, 30 minutes east — everything Phewa was twenty years ago. No tourist strip, terraced hills, a rowboat, fish for lunch at a lakeside shack. The best half-day in the valley if you want quiet.

This is the one thing the sixth trek day costs you. On the five-day plan, 13 October was pure buffer with two soft Pokhara days in front of it. Now it's the only spare day before you fly. If the trek slips 24 hours you'll arrive here on the 13th and fly out on the 14th with nothing in hand — survivable, because the Pokhara→Kathmandu leg has a road fallback (7–8 h by private car or tourist bus) and you still have the 15th and 16th in the valley. But it's the reason to keep this day genuinely unplanned.

Fly to Kathmandu → Bhaktapur flight + hotel

  • Morning flight Pokhara → Kathmandu, 25–30 minutes. Take an early slot: mountain weather is best before 10:00, and Dashain week is the single busiest travel period of the Nepali year — book this one well ahead and expect full aircraft.

Nepal 11

Last full day — Dashain in the streets

A day with no fixed shape. Some good ways to spend it:

  • Shopping, properly. Thamel and Patan for pashmina (ask for the burn test), thangka, singing bowls, Nepali tea, handmade lokta paper, a khukuri. Bargain politely; expect to land 20–30% under the opening price. Patan's Mahaguthi and the fair-trade shops around the Durbar Square are fixed-price and reliable if you'd rather not haggle.
  • A Nepali cooking class — half a day, market visit included, and you come home able to make momos and dal bhat properly.
  • The Garden of Dreams in Thamel: a restored Edwardian folly garden, an unreasonably pleasant place to read for two hours in the middle of the chaos.
  • Kirtipur — a Newar hill town on the valley rim, twenty minutes away, almost no tourists, and one of the best views back over Kathmandu.
  • Dashain is in full swing: giant bamboo swings (ping) at every crossroads, kites over the rooftops all afternoon, markets heaving, families in new clothes. Be aware that goat sacrifice is part of this festival from around 19 October — you leave before the main days, but you may see preparations.
  • Repack tonight. Weigh the bags — you will have bought more than you think.

Why this shape

Bhutan first is a gift, not an obstacle

Your fixed dates put Bhutan before Nepal, which at first looks like the wrong way round. It isn't. You finish Bhutan with the two-day Bumdra trek, sleeping at 3,800 m on 4 October. Three days later you start Mardi Himal — arriving at the trailhead already acclimatised, already walking fit, and already in the rhythm. Most people do this trek cold off a plane; you won't.

Sarangkot before the trail, Lakeside after

Sleeping at Sarangkot on arrival buys you a private sunrise and a trailhead that's thirty minutes away along the ridge instead of an hour and a half up from town. Lakeside afterwards gives you the hot showers, the massages, the bakeries and the boats — which is what you actually want on the far side of six days of teahouses.

Eleven nights, six of them walking

The trek eats 6 to 12 October. That leaves one soft day in Pokhara — which is also your buffer — and three nights back in the valley. The buffer matters: Nepali domestic flights cancel for weather, treks run a day long, and the one thing you cannot flex is TG320 at 13:30 on the 17th. Going from five walking days to six spends the spare day on the mountain, which we think is the right trade, but it is a real trade.

You arrive on one festival and leave on another

Indra Jatra (23–30 Sep) is still running on your first evening — masked dances and the Kumari in Kathmandu Durbar Square. Dashain begins 11 October, the day you walk off the mountain, and builds all week: swings, kites, marigolds, and the entire country travelling home. That last fact is the practical one — book every domestic flight and hotel from the 11th onward as early as you possibly can.
