Dashain caveat

From 11 October, hotel demand in Pokhara and Kathmandu rises sharply and some family-run places close for the main festival days. Book with free cancellation where possible, but book now rather than on arrival.

Still to arrange

The trek

Five days, 7–11 October, starting and ending at Sarangkot rather than Pokhara. We are bringing our own porter — a contact from Sarangkot — so what we need from an agency is the licensed guide and the permits.

Say this up front in the first email, because some agencies won't take an outside porter for insurance reasons and it is better to find out now than at the trailhead.

Permits — the timing problem

The Nepal Tourism Board office at Damside is open Sunday to Friday and closed Saturdays, and we are in Bhutan until the morning of 6 October, then on the road all day to Sarangkot. We cannot collect these ourselves.

So: send the agency scans of both passports and a digital passport photo each before we leave for Paro. They can issue ACAP on our behalf and hand the permits over at the Sarangkot hotel on the evening of the 6th or before we leave on the morning of the 7th. Ask for photographs of the issued permits so we know they exist.

Do not let anyone plan to "sort it at the checkpoint" — ACAP costs double there, NPR 6,000 instead of 3,000, and not every post can issue one. Carry rupees for the Machhapuchhre Rural Municipality fee, which is collected on the trail and cannot be pre-paid.

Documents and money

On paper, in a plastic wallet

  • Thai e-tickets in the Thai email · Drukair e-tickets in the Drukair email
