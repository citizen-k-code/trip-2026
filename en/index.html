The shape of it

Four movements, each with its own rhythm — and deliberately ordered so the hardest walking comes last, when you are fit and acclimatised.

1 · Two days in the Kathmandu Valley

Land, sleep off the flight, and spend one full day on the three places that matter: Boudhanath at dawn, Swayambhunath late morning, Pashupatinath at dusk. Base yourself in Patan rather than Thamel — quieter, better fed, and a five-minute walk from one of Asia's great squares.

2 · Nine days across Bhutan

West to east and back: Thimphu, over the Dochula Pass to Punakha, up into the crane valley at Phobjikha, then Paro. It finishes with the two-day Bumdra trek — a night camped at 3,800 m, then down onto Tiger's Nest from above, arriving before the crowds who climb from the car park.

3 · The Mardi Himal ridge

Five walking days on the quiet cousin of the Annapurna routes: out of subtropical forest onto an open alpine spine directly beneath Machhapuchhre, topping out at 4,200 m. We get there by road: a hired car from Kathmandu airport, two hours in the hill town of Bandipur on the way, and a night on Sarangkot before and after.

4 · Back to the valley for Dashain

Sunrise and paragliding on Sarangkot, two days by the lake in Pokhara, then a flight east for a last Himalayan sunrise at Nagarkot, a night inside Bhaktapur and a last day in Kathmandu — by now deep into Dashain, Nepal's biggest festival: bamboo swings at every crossroads and kites over the rooftops.

Why this order works

Bhutan first looks like the wrong way round, and isn't. The Bhutan leg ends with a night at 3,800 m at Bumdra. Three days later you start Mardi Himal already acclimatised, already walking fit, already in the rhythm. Most people arrive at that trailhead cold off a plane.

How high you'll be, day by day

Two treks, two peaks — and the gap between them is the whole argument for this itinerary.

Read the two peaks together

You sleep at 3,800 m at Bumdra on 4 October and touch about 3,850 m the next morning above Tiger's Nest. Five days later High Camp is 3,580 m — lower than you have already slept — and that same afternoon you walk up to 3,900 m and come back down to sleep, before the push to 4,200 m the next morning. Climb high, sleep low, twice in a row.

Kathmandu Valley

26 – 28 September · two nights in Patan

26 SepSat · Day 1

Arrive, and do almost nothing

Sleep Patan, Kathmandu · ~1,350 m
Getting in Visa on arrival, SIM at the airport, 35–50 min by taxi

Land early afternoon. Clear immigration, buy a SIM in the arrivals hall, take a prepaid taxi south to Patan.

  • Patan Durbar Square and the Patan Museum — the best museum in Nepal, a small and beautifully curated collection of Newari bronzes in the old royal palace. Last entry around 17:00.
  • Walk home past Pimbahal pond and the courtyard temples. Nothing more ambitious than that on day one.
  • Indra Jatra runs 23–30 September in 2026. The main chariot day is the 25th, but the last nights still bring masked lakhey dancers and oil lamps to Kathmandu Durbar Square, twenty minutes north.
  • Dinner: Newari, in Patan. Honacha behind the Durbar Square for the rough-and-real version; Café Swotha or Yala Mandala for a courtyard table.
27 SepSun · Day 2

The big valley day

Sleep Patan, Kathmandu · ~1,350 m
How Car and driver for the day; a guide for the morning repays itself

One day is enough for the three that matter, if you run them in this order.

  • 07:00 · Boudhanath. Walk the kora clockwise with Tibetan pilgrims while the butter lamps are still lit, then breakfast on a rooftop looking at the eyes on the stupa. The single best hour in Kathmandu.
  • 10:00 · Swayambhunath. The Monkey Temple on its hill — 365 steps, or drive round to the back entrance. Best valley panorama in the city. Keep a hand on your sunglasses.
  • 12:30 · Kathmandu Durbar Square and Thamel. Lunch, the Kumari Ghar, and a first pass through the gear shops.
  • 17:15 · Pashupatinath. The Bagmati cremation ghats and the evening aarti around 18:00–19:00. Non-Hindus can't enter the main temple; the east-bank terraces are where you want to be anyway. Do not photograph the cremations.
Repack tonight: tomorrow is Bhutan, and what you need there is not what you need on a ridge.

Bhutan

28 September – 6 October · eight nights, west to east and back

How Bhutan works

You cannot travel independently. Every visitor is with a licensed guide and driver for the whole stay, and a Sustainable Development Fee of US$100 per person per night is built into the cost — it funds free healthcare, free education and the forestry that keeps the country carbon-negative. It is not a tourist tax in the usual sense.

28 SepMon · Bhutan 1

Into Paro, on to Thimphu

Sleep Thimphu · ~2,320 m
Flight Kathmandu → Paro, 1 h 05 · sit on the left for Everest, Makalu and Kanchenjunga

The descent into Paro is the most theatrical landing in commercial aviation — a corkscrew between hillsides with the wingtips apparently in the pine trees.

  • Welcome ceremony, then the 1.5 h drive to Thimphu.
  • School of Arts and Crafts, where students learn the thirteen traditional Bhutanese crafts, with a chance to try one.
  • A consultation at the School of Astrology, and a blessing ceremony. Bring your birth time if you know it.
Bhutan runs on UTC+6 — 15 minutes ahead of Nepal. Thimphu at 2,320 m is your first altitude: drink more than feels necessary.
29 SepTue · Bhutan 2

Takins, a giant Buddha, and the Dochula Pass

Sleep Punakha · ~1,250 m
Day high Dochula Pass, ~3,100 m
  • The takin preserve — Bhutan's national animal, which looks like a bee stung a gnu.
  • Hike to Wangditse temple; prepare and offer butter lamps.
  • Buddha Dordenma, 51 m of gilded bronze above the valley.
  • Over the Dochula Pass (2.5 h), ringed by 108 chortens with the eastern Himalaya on the skyline. The view window is roughly 07:00–10:00 — worth leaving Thimphu early for.
  • Leg-stretch at Lamperi botanical park on the way down.
30 SepWed · Bhutan 3

Village life and the most beautiful building in Bhutan

Sleep Punakha · ~1,250 m
Note Punakha is warm and humid — a shorts day after two cool ones
  • A morning of rural activities at a farm homestay.
  • Hike to Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten (45 min up through rice terraces), then a picnic lunch by the river.
  • Punakha Dzong, at the confluence of the Pho Chhu and Mo Chhu — go late afternoon when the whitewash turns gold. Wearing gho and kira for the visit is a compliment to your hosts, and makes for the photograph you'll actually keep.
  • The 180 m suspension bridge, which bounces.
1 OctThu · Bhutan 4

Nuns, then up into the crane valley

Sleep Phobjikha · ~3,000 m
Walking Lawala to Kumbu nature hike, 3 h
  • Sangchen Dorji Lhuendrup nunnery, and time with the nuns.
  • Drive to Phobjikha via the Nubding valley (2.5 h), then walk down through forest into the bowl.
  • Evening prayers at Gangtey Shedra. Sitting in on the monks' chanting as the light goes is the kind of thing that stays with you.
Phobjikha is a glacial valley at 3,000 m with almost no central heating. Evenings bite — wear the fleece to prayers.
2 OctFri · Bhutan 5

Gangtey, and the black-necked cranes

Sleep Wangdue / Lobesa · ~1,350 m
Walking Gangtey Nature Trail, 1.5 h
  • Early cycling on the valley floor if you fancy it — flat, empty and beautiful at 07:00.
  • Gangtey Monastery, then the nature trail through pine forest and meadow.
  • The Crane Information Centre. The cranes fly in from Tibet from late October, so early October is early — but scouts arrive, and the telescopes and the film are worth the stop regardless.
3 OctSat · Bhutan 6

The Divine Madman, then west to Paro

Sleep Paro · ~2,280 m
Driving 3 h, back over Dochula
  • Chimi Lhakhang at Sopsokha — the fertility temple of Drukpa Kunley, the 15th-century "Divine Madman". This is where the phalluses painted on house walls across the country start making sense. It is a genuine and cheerful folk tradition, not a joke at your expense.
  • In Paro: Rinpung Dzong, the National Museum in the old watchtower, and the easy Zuri hike (1 h).
  • A sand mandala try-out — the meditative business of placing each grain.
Last real shopping stop before the trek. Repack tonight: tomorrow you climb to 3,800 m and sleep there.
4 OctSun · Bhutan 7

Bumdra, day one — up to the sky-burial ridge

Sleep Bumdra camp · ~3,800 m
Walking 6–7 h, ~+1,100 m

Up from Sangchokhor monastery through blue pine and rhododendron to a meadow at 3,800 m. Hoist a prayer flag at the sky-burial site. Camp under the stars.

  • Carry a litre and a half and your shell — afternoon cloud builds fast on this ridge.
  • Nights drop to around freezing. Thermals, hat and the down layer come out of the duffel tonight.
  • The Milky Way from that meadow is absurd.
5 OctMon · Bhutan 8

Down onto Tiger's Nest — from above

Sleep Paro · ~2,280 m
Walking 3–4 h, mostly down

Descend the ridge to arrive at Paro Taktsang from over the top, ahead of everyone climbing up from the car park. This is the best way to do Tiger's Nest, and the reason the Bumdra trek is worth its two days.

  • No bags, phones or cameras inside the monastery — lockers at the cafeteria. Shoot everything on the approach.
  • Afterwards: a traditional hot stone bath, ideally the outdoor kind by a stream rather than the hotel version. It is the last hot water before a week of teahouses.
  • Paro town for a stroll, and the Namgay artisanal brewery if you're interested.

By road to the Annapurnas

6 October · Kathmandu → Bandipur → Sarangkot, by hired car

Why the road and not the plane

Flying Kathmandu–Pokhara the same morning you land from Paro means an international-to-domestic connection on two famously weather-prone airports. A car from Nepal Vehicle Hiring (vehiclehiringnepal.com), waiting at arrivals, takes all of that out: no second check-in, no 20 kg limit, no cancellation risk the day before a trek — and it puts Bandipur, which you cannot fly to, right on the route. The price is a long day: around seven hours of driving, the last stretch after dark.

6 OctTue · Nepal 1

Paro, Bandipur, Sarangkot — three places in one day

Sleep Sarangkot, above Pokhara · ~1,590 m
Flight Paro → Kathmandu, 1 h · sit on the right · then ~7 h by car
  • Land at 08:10. Nepali tourist visas are multiple-entry, so you re-enter on the one you already hold — no new fee. Draw the trek cash at the airport machines now; there is no better chance before the trailhead.
  • ~09:30 · On the road. The driver meets you at arrivals. Out of the valley over the rim at Naubise, then down the Prithvi Highway along the Trishuli river — rafting camps, suspension bridges, lorries — to Mugling and on to Dumre. About four hours.
  • ~13:30 · Bandipur, two hours. Up a winding side road to a ridge saddle at about 1,030 m. Lunch, the bazaar, and the viewpoint (below).
  • ~15:30 · On to Pokhara, about two and a half hours, then the switchbacks up to Sarangkot. Expect to arrive around dark. Dinner, bags sorted into "trek" and "stays here", bed early.
Bandipur in two hours. A Newar trading town on the old India–Tibet route, which emptied when the Prithvi Highway was built along the valley floor in the 1970s — and was saved by it: no through traffic, so the main bazaar is car-free and still lined with brick-and-timber merchant houses from the 18th and 19th centuries. Walk the bazaar end to end, look into the Bindabasini temple, and climb ten minutes to Tundikhel, the old parade ground on the ridge, for a view that on a clear day runs from Dhaulagiri past the Annapurnas to Langtang. Lunch on a balcony over the street.

The Mardi Himal trek

7 – 11 October · five walking days, 1,770 m to 4,200 m

Mardi Himal is the quiet cousin of the Annapurna circuit routes: a single high ridge that climbs out of subtropical forest onto an open alpine spine directly beneath Machhapuchhre. There is no technical ground — steep, rooty forest walking at the bottom and a bare, exposed crest at the top. Teahouses the whole way.

Five days, and what that costs

Five days works because you arrive acclimatised from Bhutan: Bumdra put you at 3,800 m only three days before the trailhead. The price is day 2, which now runs from Forest Camp all the way to Badal Danda — +690 m and the longest climb of the trek. In return the short hop to High Camp on day 3 keeps the most important part of the old plan: a free afternoon to walk up to View Point at 3,900 m and come back down to sleep at 3,580 m, the night before the summit morning. If either of you feels the altitude on day 2, stop at Low Camp (2,970 m) instead — it's on the way.

7 OctWed · Trek 1

Sunrise on Sarangkot, then into the forest

Sleep Forest Camp · 2,520 m
Night ~8–15 °C
  • 05:45 · Sunrise from the terrace, if you can face it after yesterday. Annapurna II, IV, III, Gangapurna, Annapurna South, Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre light up left to right over twenty minutes.
  • ~08:30 · Drive to Kande (1,770 m), 30 minutes west along the ridge. The main bags stay at the Sarangkot hotel. ACAP checkpoint at the trailhead.
  • Stone steps up to Australian Camp (2,060 m) — teahouse terrace, first full panorama, worth the tea stop — then through Pothana and Deurali, where the trail leaves the Annapurna Base Camp route and turns north into oak and rhododendron forest.
  • A long gentle climb under canopy to Forest Camp: six to eight teahouses in a clearing, no views, all atmosphere.
Easy first day by design: +750 m over 4–5 hours with a real lunch stop. Fill bottles at Australian Camp and treat the water — from Kande up it's tablets or a filter, not bought plastic.
8 OctThu · Trek 2

Out of the trees and up onto the spine

Sleep Badal Danda · 3,210 m
Walking 5–6 h · +690 m, the big climb
  • Three to four hours of ridge forest through Rest Camp. The canopy thins and the rhododendrons get gnarled and wind-bent.
  • Low Camp (2,970 m) for lunch, and the moment the mountain shows itself: Machhapuchhre fills the entire northern sky, unreasonably close, with Annapurna South and Hiunchuli to the left.
  • After lunch the forest gives out and you step onto a narrow open crest with drops on both sides, dwarf rhododendron underfoot. Badal Danda means "cloud hill", and earns it: you are now at the height where the afternoon cloud sea forms below you.
Altitude from here on: drink 3–4 litres a day, eat more than you feel like, and mention any headache rather than toughing it out. Go slowly after Low Camp — this is the day to be patient.
9 OctFri · Trek 3

High Camp by lunch, then climb high and sleep low

Sleep High Camp · 3,580 m
Night −2 to +4 °C · a proper cold night
  • Two hours along the crest to High Camp, the last permanent teahouse on the route. Basic rooms, a dining hut with a stove, one of the great teahouse views in Nepal.
  • The afternoon matters: walk the first hour of tomorrow's route up to View Point, about 3,900 m, then come back down and sleep 320 m lower. You touch a new high, you sleep low, and you see the summit path in daylight before walking it in the dark.
  • Agree the start time over dinner, order breakfast in advance, lay out your layers where you can find them at half four.
10 OctSat · Trek 4

Upper View Point, 4,200 m

Sleep Badal Danda · 3,210 m
Walking 6–8 h · the longest day
  • 04:30 · Head torches on. Steeply at first, then along the bare spine you walked yesterday afternoon — which you will be glad of.
  • ~06:00 · View Point (3,900 m) for sunrise. Machhapuchhre catches first, then Annapurna South and Hiunchuli, then the whole wall. Behind you the Pokhara valley is a lake of cloud with hills poking through.
  • ~08:00 · Upper View Point (4,200 m). Machhapuchhre's twin fishtail summit is about 2 km away and 2.5 km above you, close enough to see the flutings in the ice.
  • Optional: Mardi Himal Base Camp, 4,500 m — two hours out and back over easy but airy ground. Only if you're both feeling strong; on a five-day plan it makes a very long day.
  • Then down to High Camp for a late breakfast, and on to Badal Danda for the night.
Weather rule. If the morning is socked in, go as far as View Point and decide over breakfast rather than burning yourself out. The mountain will not be photographed through cloud.
11 OctSun · Trek 5

Down the far side to Siding, and back up to Sarangkot

Sleep Sarangkot · 1,590 m
Walking 4–5 h down, then ~2½ h by jeep
  • The eastern descent is a completely different landscape from the way up: steep zig-zags through forest, then terraced millet and rice, then the Gurung village of Siding (1,750 m) with its slate roofs and buffalo. Hard on the knees — poles earn their place today.
  • Jeep down a rough road to Pokhara and straight up to Sarangkot, where the bags are waiting.
  • Sunset over Phewa Lake from the hotel terrace, a long shower, and the first bed in five nights.
Dashain begins today — Ghatasthapana, the first day of Nepal's biggest festival. You come down off the mountain straight into it.

Pokhara, and back to the valley

12 – 17 October · paragliding, the lake, Nagarkot, Bhaktapur and Dashain in the streets

12 OctMon · Nepal 7

Sunrise, then off the hill by paraglider

Sleep Pokhara, Lakeside · 820 m
Fly 09:00–12:00, tandem
  • 05:45 · Sunrise from Sarangkot — this time with nothing to rush off to. The Annapurnas left to right, and the mountain you were standing under two days ago.
  • Paragliding from Sarangkot — one of the top three sites on earth, and you're already at the take-off. Thirty minutes tandem runs about US$90–100, an hour US$110–150, and you land on the shore of Phewa Lake. Only with an NCAA-licensed operator and a CIVL-certified pilot; book a day ahead.
  • The bags go down by taxi to Lakeside. Afternoon: shower again, laundry in, a cold Everest beer with the boats in front of you.
Morning flights are the calm ones. If the wind or cloud is wrong, try again tomorrow from Lakeside — the operators drive you up.
13 OctTue · Nepal 8

Pokhara — a day off

Sleep Pokhara, Lakeside · 820 m
Plan Deliberately loose

Recovery, and slack in case the paragliding was weathered off. Pick two of these and leave the rest.

  • Row across Phewa Lake to Tal Barahi temple on its island, then up to the World Peace Pagoda (45 min on foot); come down by taxi.
  • An hour of massage in Lakeside. Your calves will otherwise file a formal complaint.
  • The International Mountain Museum — much better than it sounds the day after a trek.
  • Begnas Lake, 30 minutes east: everything Phewa was twenty years ago.
14 OctWed · Nepal 9

Fly east, and up to the valley rim

Sleep Nagarkot · ~2,195 m
Flight Pokhara → Kathmandu, 25–30 min · afternoon, still to book · sit on the left
  • A slow morning by the lake, then the afternoon flight east. Sit on the left for a last look at the Annapurnas, Manaslu and Ganesh Himal.
  • Drive straight out to Nagarkot, about an hour east of the airport. No crossing Kathmandu, no traffic.
  • Nagarkot sits at 2,195 m on the valley rim and looks out over eight Himalayan ranges — Ganesh Himal, Langtang Lirung, Dorje Lakpa, Gauri Shankar, and on a clear October morning a distant Everest.
The afternoon flight is the risk. Pokhara weather builds through the day and afternoon services are the ones that get delayed or cancelled. If it goes, airlines rebook you onto the first flight next morning — you lose the Nagarkot sunrise but not Bhaktapur. Take the earliest afternoon slot you can.
15 OctThu · Nepal 10

Sunrise east, then sleep in a medieval city

Sleep Bhaktapur old town · ~1,400 m
Drive Nagarkot → Bhaktapur, ~45 min
  • Sunrise from Nagarkot — the eastern Himalaya this time, the mirror of Sarangkot. Pick a hotel whose rooms face the range, so it happens from your own balcony.
  • Down the hill to Bhaktapur, 45 minutes. On the way, or as a short detour, Changunarayan — the oldest temple in the valley, on its own ridge.
  • Sleep inside the old city. That's the trick: day-trippers arrive at ten and leave at five, and the town in between — at dusk, at dawn, lit by doorway lamps — is the finest surviving Newari city in the valley.
  • Evening: Durbar Square and the Golden Gate, then Taumadhi Square under the five-tiered Nyatapola temple.
16 OctFri · Nepal 11

Bhaktapur at dawn, then a last day in the city

Sleep Kathmandu or Patan · ~1,350 m
Festival Dashain is five days in
  • 06:30 — walk the empty squares before the ticket gates open. Pottery Square with the morning's pots drying in rows, the wood-carving lanes, the Peacock Window in Dattatreya Square.
  • Eat the juju dhau, Bhaktapur's "king of yoghurts", in its clay bowl. Genuinely one of the best things in Nepal.
  • Midday into Kathmandu. Shopping — pashmina, thangka, singing bowls, tea, lokta paper — or the Garden of Dreams, or a cooking class, or the hill town of Kirtipur on the valley rim.
  • Dashain is everywhere: giant bamboo swings at crossroads, kites over the rooftops, markets heaving, families in new clothes.
Weigh the bags tonight. You will have bought more than you think.
17 OctSat · Day 23

Home

Sleep In the air
Leave for the airport three hours before, not two
  • Today is Phulpati, the seventh day of Dashain, with processions in central Kathmandu — traffic is unpredictable, so add half an hour.
  • Tribhuvan departures are slow: multiple security screenings, manual checks, no jet bridge.
  • Spend your last rupees airside. You cannot change them outside Nepal, and the exchange desks want the original ATM receipts.
